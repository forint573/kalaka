# Cold Start — The Density Playbook

Your stated first goal: **make the app densely populated in small local communities that already have sellers.** This is the hardest problem in the project and the one most likely to kill it. It is also mostly not a software problem.

---

## The governing rule

> **Recruit supply first. Demand follows supply — never the reverse.**

Twenty producers with nothing to browse is a boring app. Twenty buyers with nothing to buy is a dead one, and they never come back. In food, supply creates its own demand: people want the eggs.

## Human onboarding, not self-serve

A 70-year-old baker will not download an app, register, write a listing, photograph her bread and set a pickup radius. **She never will, and no amount of UX polish changes that.**

**The steward model.** One person per community — paid or stipended, local, known, trusted — whose job is to sit at the kitchen table and do it *with* people:

- create the profile, take the photographs, write the first listing
- teach the one voice command that matters: *"I baked twenty breads."*
- show up again in week two, when it has gone quiet
- mediate the first dispute before it becomes village gossip
- capture the first knowledge interviews

This is the single highest-leverage line item in the budget, and it is exactly the kind of thing grants fund happily. Budget for it as staff, not as marketing.

## Sequence for community #1

**Choose one commune. Name it.** Nothing else can be planned until this is decided — pick for existing production density, a cooperative *primărie*, and someone local who will champion it.

1. **Weeks 1–4 · Recruit 15–25 producers by hand.** Bread, eggs, milk, honey, vegetables, meat, wool, wood, repairs. Face to face, through the steward. No public launch.
2. **Weeks 3–5 · Seed the knowledge layer.** Four or five elder interviews, wood-oven bread first. Now the app has something in it besides listings.
3. **Week 5 · Seed the feed.** Forty-odd real posts before anyone else arrives. **Never open an empty app.**
4. **Week 6 · Launch at the weekly market or a village event.** Profiles created on the spot, phone in hand. Not a download link — a person with a tablet.
5. **Weeks 6–12 · Work the loop.** The steward chases the first reservations, the first collections, the first confirmations. Fix what breaks in person.
6. **Only then, expand** — and only to a neighbouring commune, ideally one that shares a market day.

## Anchor institutions

Rural distribution is not digital marketing. It is:

- the ***primărie*** — legitimacy, and the notice board everyone reads
- the **church** — still the highest-attendance weekly gathering in most Szekler villages
- the **school** — parents, and grandchildren who become the helper accounts
- **market day** — where the producers and buyers already physically are
- the **existing village Facebook group and its admin** — this is the current platform, and its admin is the single most valuable ally in the community
- the **local shop, mill and agricultural supplier**

## Expansion gate

Do not expand on enthusiasm. Expand on evidence. Define the numbers before launch and hold to them — something in the shape of:

- N producers posting availability in a typical week
- M buyers who have collected **more than once** — repeat is the only signal that matters
- a working knowledge layer with real workshops happening
- the loop running without the steward pushing it

Until commune #1 clears the gate, **a second commune is a distraction.** One village that genuinely works is the entire proof; five half-alive villages prove nothing and burn the goodwill you cannot re-earn.

## Motivating the demand side

- **Discovery beats persuasion.** Nobody needs convincing to want fresh eggs 800 m away — they need to know they exist. Most of the work is visibility.
- **The first purchase must be trivially easy** — reserve, walk, pay cash. No account depth, no payment setup.
- **Seasonal moments are the best acquisition events** — Easter, Christmas baking, plum harvest, pig season. Plan launches around them.
- **Contribution stories over commerce**: *"Mária baked for 38 families this month."*

## Motivating new producers

Straight from [app-logic.md §6](app-logic.md): the knowledge layer is the top of the funnel. Someone learns, practises, then offers.

Also:
- **Lower the threshold below "business."** "I have a dozen spare eggs this week" must be as legitimate a listing as a commercial operation. Most first-time producers will never be businesses, and that is fine — they are the density.
- **Mark first offerings publicly.** Village-scale encouragement is powerful and costs nothing.
- **Requests reveal latent supply.** Seeing "anyone selling duck eggs?" three times is how a person discovers they have something to sell.
- **Show the neighbour succeeding.** Your own core hypothesis: visible local success is the strongest motivator there is. Make it visible — carefully, without turning it into a leaderboard.

## Measure the community, not the app

Per community, not aggregate. Aggregate numbers hide dead villages behind one live one.

- weekly active producers posting availability
- reservations completed, and the **repeat rate**
- first-time offerers per month
- workshops held and attended
- share of producers still active after 3 and 6 months — the number that actually tells you whether this works
