# Knowledge Layer — Generational Transfer

Working name: **Kaláka Tudás** (HU, "knowledge"). Romanian name needs a native speaker — *Meșteșug* ("craft") is a candidate, not a decision.

The third pillar, equal to marketplace and feed. Not a content library bolted onto a marketplace.

---

## Why it is a pillar

Three separate jobs, one mechanism:

1. **It is the supply funnel.** People who learn to bake become people who sell bread. See [app-logic.md §6](app-logic.md).
2. **It is the strongest non-transactional reason to open the app** — content that has nothing to do with buying anything, which is exactly what defeats bypass.
3. **It is the thing that is actually being lost.** The bread knowledge is in people who are in their seventies. This is a capture window that closes, and it will not reopen. Of everything in this project, this is the part with a deadline.

## The capture problem

An 74-year-old who bakes extraordinary bread in a wood oven **will not write a tutorial.** They will not film themselves, storyboard a video, or fill in a recipe form. Any design that assumes they will produces an empty library.

**The assistant is the scribe.** This is a far better use of the voice layer than inventory management:

> "Tell me how you make the bread with potatoes."

The assistant listens, transcribes, and — critically — **asks the follow-up questions the elder would never think to volunteer**, because to them it is too obvious to mention:

- *How do you know the oven is ready?* → "when you can hold your hand in for as long as a Miatyánk"
- *How much potato?* → "as much as fits in your two hands"
- *How do you know the dough is done rising?*
- *What did you learn this from — who taught you?*

That tacit layer is the whole value. It is precisely what a written recipe loses, and precisely what an interviewer can extract. Then it is structured, transcribed, translated, and returned to the elder for approval before anything is published.

**Capture modes, in order of realism:** voice interview (assistant-led) · video from a phone held by a grandchild · photos with spoken captions · in-person workshop recorded by a steward · written, last and least.

## Content types

| Type | Shape | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Method record** | Structured: materials, ratios, tools, timing, doneness cues, common failures | The core object. Not a blog post. |
| **Video** | Short, phone-shot, third-party hosted | We do not run video infrastructure. |
| **In-person workshop** | Bookable, capacity-limited, at someone's home or the village hall | The real mechanism of transfer. |
| **Seasonal note** | "It is time to ___" | Drives annual recurring engagement. |
| **Question** | "My dough never rises in a cold kitchen" | Answered by elders — high-value, low-effort participation. |

## In-person workshops are the point

Video is the index; the actual transfer happens in a kitchen.

> **Four places. Saturday morning. My kitchen. Bring an apron.**

Bookable, capacity-limited, free or a small materials fee. This produces real-world meetings between neighbors — which *is* the north star, not a proxy for it. It also gives elders a role with standing attached, and it is the most fundable single feature in the project (see [funding-and-governance.md](funding-and-governance.md)).

## Lineage and credit

Knowledge carries attribution: **who knows this, and who did they learn it from.** *"Learned from my grandmother, Csíkszereda, around 1961."*

This matters culturally, it is a genuine reputation signal, and it makes the archive an inheritance rather than a content feed. Over time the lineage graph is itself a cultural record worth having.

## Consent and ownership — decide before capture, not after

Recording an elder's life knowledge raises real questions, and getting this wrong in a small community is unrecoverable:

- **The knowledge belongs to the person who gave it.** Explicit, informed consent at capture, in their own language, in plain terms — not a checkbox.
- **Not everything should be public.** Support family-private and village-only visibility. Some recipes are not meant to leave a house, and asking for them publicly is a real offense.
- **Right to withdraw**, exercisable by the person or their family.
- **Approval before publication**, always.
- Kaláka gives **no food-safety advice** and certifies nothing; traditional method records are cultural documentation, and anything touching preservation, curing or canning needs a plain safety note.
- **Open question:** should the archive be openly licensed as a public cultural good, given public funding? Strong argument in favor. Needs a decision with the funders and the contributors, not by us alone.

## Link into the rest of the app

Everything connects back, in both directions:

- a method record links to producers who **sell** the thing → knowledge creates demand
- a producer profile shows what they can **teach** → sellers gain a second role
- a materials list links to nearby suppliers → flour, starter, firewood, wool
- finishing a course prompts the **Learn → Practice → Offer** funnel
- workshops appear in the feed as local events

## Phase 1 scope

Do not build a learning platform. Build:

- method records with voice capture and assistant-led interviewing
- photo and short video attachment
- bookable in-person workshops
- questions and elder answers
- **one flagship subject, done properly: wood-oven bread.** The whole thing — the oven, the wood, the potatoes, the timing, three elders' versions of it, and the workshops to go with it.

Not in Phase 1: certificates, structured curricula, progress tracking, quizzes, paid courses, our own video hosting.
